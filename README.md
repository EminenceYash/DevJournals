# DevJournals

A record of what I learn, build, break, and understand along the way.

This repository is where I keep track of my technical journey in a
simple way --- not as polished notes, but as a running record of the
things that genuinely caught my attention and made me dig deeper.

## Why I Keep This

I don't want learning to become a collection of tutorials I've watched
or concepts I've memorised.

I want to be able to look back and see:

-   what I was learning at a particular point in time
-   what I struggled with
-   what suddenly clicked
-   what I built while learning
-   how my way of thinking changed
-   which ideas were worth going deeper into

Some entries may be about programming. Others might be about computer
science, problem solving, systems, tools, or something completely
unexpected that I found interesting that day.

The goal is simple:

> **Learn something. Understand it. Apply it. Write it down. Repeat.**

## What You'll Find Here

The journals will usually contain things like:

-   **Daily learning** --- concepts, topics, and discoveries from the
    day
-   **Experiments** --- small programs, tests, and things I tried just
    to see what happens
-   **Problem solving** --- problems I worked through and what I learned
    from them
-   **Projects** --- progress, mistakes, decisions, and lessons from
    things I'm building
-   **Reflections** --- observations about learning, discipline,
    communication, teamwork, and working better
-   **Rabbit holes** --- those random questions that start with *"wait,
    how does that actually work?"*

Not every day will have something impressive.

That's intentional.

The point of this repository is to preserve the process, not just the
highlights.

## My Learning Principle

I'm trying to move from:

`Learning → Remembering`

towards:

`Learning → Understanding → Applying → Reflecting`

Knowing that something works is useful.

Knowing **why** it works is better.

Being able to build something with it is better still.

## Journal Structure

Entries are organised chronologically so the repository becomes a
timeline of my progress.

``` text
DevJournals/
│
├── 2026/
│   ├── 09-September/
│   └── ...
│
└── README.md
```

The structure may evolve as the journal grows. I care more about keeping
the record than keeping the structure perfect.

## A Small Rule

I don't want to rewrite my past self.

If I misunderstood something, struggled with something obvious, wrote
something badly, or got excited about a tiny concept --- it stays.

Those details are part of the journey.

> **Progress is easier to appreciate when you keep evidence of where you
> started.**
